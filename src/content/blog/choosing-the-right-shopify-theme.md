Choosing a theme is one of the first decisions you make on Shopify, and it can be a lot of work to change later. Switching themes after launch can mean rebuilding pages, re-checking apps and re-testing everything.

So it's worth taking time to choose properly. Here's how I'd approach it.

## Start with your products, not the demo

Theme demo stores are designed to look amazing. They use polished photography, carefully chosen products and just the right amount of text. Your store may not look like that on day one, and that's fine.

Instead of asking "which demo looks best?", ask:

- **How many products do I have?** A store with 15 products needs a very different layout from one with 1,500.
- **What do customers need to compare?** Sizes, colors, materials, ingredients?
- **What's my main selling point?** Beautiful visuals, technical details, price, or a story behind the brand?

The right theme is the one that suits *your* catalog and *your* customers.

## When a free theme is enough

Shopify's free themes are a solid starting point, and they're built by Shopify itself. A free theme is often the best choice when:

- your catalog is small or medium-sized,
- your branding is simple and clean,
- you'd rather spend your budget on photography, content or marketing,
- speed matters more than special layouts.

A free theme with a few thoughtful customizations can go a long way.

## When a paid theme is worth it

Paid themes earn their price when you need features built in that would otherwise require apps or custom code, for example:

- advanced filtering for large catalogs,
- mega menus with images,
- lookbooks, shoppable images or detailed size charts,
- particular layouts that match your brand's style.

Every feature that's built into the theme is one less app to install, and fewer apps usually means a faster, more reliable store.

## A quick evaluation checklist

Before choosing a theme, check:

1. **Mobile first.** Open the demo on your phone before anything else.
2. **Speed.** Test the demo store's speed on mobile, keeping in mind your real store will have your own images and apps.
3. **Editing.** Can you change sections, colors and text yourself in the theme editor without calling a developer?
4. **Updates and support.** Is the theme updated regularly? Do the developers answer questions?
5. **Reviews.** What do other store owners say, especially the negative reviews?
6. **The features you actually need.** Not the features that look nice in the demo.

## A common mistake

Choosing a theme for one flashy feature, then installing several apps to add everything else it's missing. You can end up with a slower store, monthly app bills and a design held together with patches.

It's usually better to choose a theme that covers most of what you need, then add the rest with careful customization.
