Choosing a theme is one of the first decisions you make on Shopify, and one of the hardest to undo later. Switching themes after launch means rebuilding pages, re-checking apps and re-testing everything.

So it's worth taking an hour to choose properly. Here's how I approach it with clients.

## Start with your products, not the demo

Theme demo stores are designed to look amazing. They use perfect photography, carefully chosen products and exactly the right amount of text. Your store won't look like that on day one, and that's fine.

Instead of asking "which demo looks best?", ask:

- **How many products do I have?** A store with 15 products needs a very different layout from one with 1,500.
- **What do customers need to compare?** Sizes, colours, materials, ingredients?
- **What's my main selling point?** Beautiful visuals, technical details, price, or a story behind the brand?

The right theme is the one that suits *your* catalogue and *your* customers.

## When a free theme is enough

Shopify's free themes are genuinely good now. They're fast, well maintained and work smoothly with Shopify's newest features. A free theme is often the best choice when:

- your catalogue is small or medium-sized,
- your branding is simple and clean,
- you'd rather spend your budget on photography, content or marketing,
- speed matters more than special layouts.

Many successful stores run on free themes with a few thoughtful customisations.

## When a paid theme is worth it

Paid themes earn their price when you need features built in that would otherwise require apps or custom code, for example:

- advanced filtering for large catalogues,
- mega menus with images,
- lookbooks, shoppable images or detailed size charts,
- particular layouts that match your brand's style.

Every feature that's built into the theme is one less app to install, and fewer apps usually means a faster, more reliable store.

## My quick evaluation checklist

Before recommending a theme, I check:

1. **Mobile first.** I open the demo on my phone before anything else.
2. **Speed.** I test the demo store's speed on mobile.
3. **Editing.** Can you change sections, colours and text yourself in the theme editor without calling a developer?
4. **Updates and support.** Is the theme updated regularly? Do the developers answer questions?
5. **Reviews.** What do other store owners say, especially the negative reviews?
6. **The features I actually need.** Not the features that look nice in the demo.

## The mistake I see most often

Choosing a theme for one flashy feature, then installing six apps to add everything else it's missing. You end up with a slow store, monthly app bills and a design held together with patches.

It's usually better to choose a theme that covers 90% of what you need, then add the last 10% with careful customisation.
