Every extra second your store takes to load gives shoppers a reason to leave. The good news: most slow Shopify stores have the same few problems, and you can fix many of them yourself.

## 1. Shrink your images before uploading

Large photos are the most common reason a store feels slow. A product photo straight from a camera or phone can be several megabytes.

- Resize images to the size they are actually shown (around 2000 px wide is plenty for most product photos).
- Save them as compressed JPG or WebP files.
- Use free tools like Squoosh or TinyPNG before uploading.

## 2. Remove apps you no longer use

Many apps add their own code to every page, and some leave it behind even after you uninstall them. Go through your apps list and remove anything you don't actively use. If a store still feels heavy afterwards, the theme files may need a cleanup.

## 3. Keep your homepage focused

Five sliders, three videos and a dozen sections look impressive in the editor, but every section adds weight. Keep the sections that help people shop, and remove the rest.

## 4. Be careful with fonts

Every extra font and font weight is another file to download. Stick to one or two font families, and use the fonts built into your theme where possible.

## 5. Test on a real phone

Most of your visitors are on mobile, often on slower connections. Open your store on your own phone, on mobile data, and click through a full purchase. If anything feels slow or clumsy to you, it feels the same to your customers.
