## The challenge for a store like this

People like to touch bedding before buying it. Sheets and duvets are also higher-priced, considered purchases. The store has to replace "feeling the fabric" with enough confidence that customers buy anyway, and ideally buy a full set rather than one piece.

## Homepage walkthrough

1. **Seasonal hook first.** A gift guide announcement and a new product launch ("Now Serving: New Dreamweave Waffle") keep the top of the page fresh.
2. **Bundle & Save.** Right away, the store pushes complete sets, raising the average order value.
3. **Shop by Category.** Clear entry points into bed, bath and other categories.
4. **FSA/HSA eligible.** A message that answers a payment question many shoppers wouldn't think to ask, and gives some a reason to buy now.
5. **New & Trending.** Social proof for undecided shoppers.
6. **"Why Brooklinen?"** One section stacks every trust signal: premium materials, the *365-Day Happiness Guarantee*, fast free shipping and *175k+ 5-star reviews*.
7. **In the News** and **Come Say Hi, IRL**: press mentions plus physical stores for people who want to touch the product.
8. **Get on the List.** Email signup to bring visitors back.

## Catalog and navigation

- The catalog is organized by **product type** (Sheets, Towels, Blankets, Robes, Shams, Duvet Covers, Quilts), which maps directly to how people shop for a bedroom or bathroom.
- **Many products are actually "content" entries.** A large share of the public catalog has the product type *Content*. This is a common trick: using product records to power promotional tiles and campaign blocks.
- **250+ collections** cover categories, best sellers, fabric families (like Breezeweave), sales and "last call" items.

## Products and merchandising

- Most products offer **colour and size**, sometimes extra options like weight or fill.
- **Product families** (for example Dreamweave) group sets, covers and shams so shoppers can complete the look.
- **"Last Call"** versions of products help clear old stock without discounting the main range.

## Trust and conversion

- **Guarantee, shipping and reviews** are repeated across the store, not hidden in policies.
- **Yotpo** powers reviews (and potentially loyalty), the backbone of the "175k+ reviews" claim.
- **A wishlist (Swym)** lets considered buyers save items and come back later.
- **Gladly** suggests a strong customer support setup, which matters for higher-priced orders.

## Ideas I'd test

These are my own suggestions, not facts about the store:

- Fabric comparison blocks (feel, warmth, weave) on product pages for first-time buyers.
- Repeating the 365-day guarantee right next to the Add to Cart button.
- A "build your bed" bundle builder that shows the savings in real time.

## What this teaches for your own case studies

- Show **how trust was built** when the product is hard to judge online.
- Explain **how bundles and product families raise order value**.
- Mention **operational details** (support, returns, wishlist), because they show you think beyond design.
