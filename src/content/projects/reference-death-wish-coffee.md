## The challenge for a store like this

Coffee is a repeat purchase in a crowded market. The real goal isn't the first sale; it's getting people to come back every few weeks. The store needs a reason to visit again, an easy way to reorder and a brand people actually want to be part of.

## Homepage walkthrough

1. **A seasonal drop as the hero.** "Back from the Gingerdead" introduces a limited flavour with a playful, on-brand headline.
2. **Energy-drink-style messaging.** "When life surges, so should you" keeps the bold voice going.
3. **Event-like launches.** "The spirits have been summoned" makes a product launch feel like a happening.
4. **New Arrivals.** A grid mixing coffee, pods, drinkware and merch, so every visit shows something new.

## Catalog and navigation

- The catalog mixes **coffee (about 37 products)** with **apparel (about 38)**, accessories, mugs, drinkware and brewing gear. Merch is as big as coffee.
- **25+ coffee bundles** plus general bundles make it easy to buy more in one go.
- **126 collections** include anniversaries, athlete and brand collaborations, landing-page carousels and flash sales, a sign of a busy campaign calendar.

## Products and merchandising

- Products are offered by **size** and **type**, so the same roast fits different brewing habits (ground, whole bean, single-serve pods).
- **Limited and seasonal flavours** create urgency and repeat visits.
- **Merch** turns customers into fans and raises the order value.

## Trust and conversion

- The store description highlights **USDA Organic and Fair Trade Certified** beans and a brand around **since 2012**: credibility in two lines.
- **Reviews (Bazaarvoice)** support the strong flavour claims with real customer feedback.
- **Shop Pay** makes checkout faster, especially for returning customers.

## Retention: the real engine

- **Rewards (Smile.io)** give customers a reason to stick with the brand.
- **Upsells (Rebuy)** suggest add-ons like mugs or pods in the cart.
- **Email and SMS (Klaviyo)** announce drops and bring people back.
- **Subscriptions** (mentioned on the site) remove the need to remember to reorder.

## Ideas I'd test

These are my own suggestions, not facts about the store:

- A "find your roast" quiz for first-time visitors.
- Showing subscription savings directly on product cards.
- A "complete your setup" bundle (coffee + mug + stir stick) in the cart.

## What this teaches for your own case studies

- For repeat-purchase products, **focus on retention**: subscriptions, rewards, email.
- Show how the **design supports the brand's personality**.
- Explain the **campaign structure**: drops, collabs and collections working together.
