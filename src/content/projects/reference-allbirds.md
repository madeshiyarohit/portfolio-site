## The challenge for a store like this

Buying shoes online is risky for the customer: *Will they fit? Will they be comfortable?* On top of that, Allbirds sells the same models in many colours and sole combinations, for men and women. The store has to keep a big, repetitive catalog easy to browse while convincing people to buy without trying the shoes on.

## Homepage walkthrough

1. **Instant split by audience.** The first choices are *Men's Shoes*, *Women's Shoes* and *Apparel & Accessories*, so nobody has to scroll through products that aren't for them.
2. **Social proof as a starting point.** Sections like *Customer Favorites* and *Popular Picks* help undecided visitors start with what others already love.
3. **A short brand line.** *"Wildly Comfortable. Super Natural."* captures the whole brand promise in four words.
4. **New arrivals.** A dedicated new-in section gives returning customers something fresh.

## Catalog and navigation

- **Product names carry the details.** Titles follow a clear pattern: *audience + model + colour + sole*, for example "Women's Cruiser Jersey - Soft Eucalyptus (Blizzard Sole)". This makes search and filtering work well.
- **Each colour is its own product.** Instead of one product with 20 colour variants, colours are separate products. That gives every colourway its own page, photos and reviews, and it's great for search engines.
- **Collections do a lot of work.** 250+ collections (including sale and campaign collections) let the team build landing pages for any audience, promotion or season without changing the site structure.

## Products and merchandising

- The only option on most products is **size**, which keeps the buying decision simple.
- Small **add-on products** (for example package protection) sit in the catalog so they can be offered at checkout.
- Outlet and discount collections keep promotions organized without cluttering the main catalog.

## Trust and conversion

- **Free shipping and a comfort-focused guarantee** answer the biggest worry about buying shoes online.
- **Loop**, a returns and exchanges app, suggests a self-service returns flow, so swapping a size is easy. That makes people more willing to buy in the first place.
- **SMS marketing (Attentive)** brings customers back for launches and sales.

## Ideas I'd test

These are my own suggestions, not facts about the store:

- A short "find your fit" helper on product pages, especially for first-time buyers.
- Side-by-side comparison of similar models (Runner vs Dasher, for example).
- Showing more customer photos in reviews, so people can see the shoes being worn.

## What this teaches for your own case studies

- Describe the **customer's biggest worry** and how the store removes it.
- Explain **catalog decisions** (naming, variants, collections), because clients rarely think about them, but they make a huge difference.
- Mention the **apps and why they were chosen**, not just their names.
